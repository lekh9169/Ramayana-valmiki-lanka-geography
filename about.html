Method and sources

This is an independent, reproducible study. It asks whether the geography and the sky described in the Valmiki Ramayana fit a real place and time. It tests that against measured data, and says plainly where the data cannot decide.

How it was done

  • The text. The geographic and astronomical verses were collected from the IIT Kanpur / RSVP edition and checked against the Baroda Critical Edition. That separates the older layer of the poem from later additions.
  • The seafloor. GEBCO 2026 at 15 arc-seconds gives the depth of the land bridge, the shape of Adam's Bridge, coastlines at any sea level and a scan of the Chagos–Laccadive ridge.
  • Sea level. A regional curve for the last 21,000 years (Lambeck et al. 2014 and Indian Ocean records), and Bintanja & van de Wal (2008) for the last 3 million years.
  • The sky, historical edition. JPL DE441 positions every day from 13,000 BCE to 1 BCE, with a sidereal zodiac anchored on Spica.
  • The sky, Treta-Yuga edition. A REBOUND N-body integration from JPL DE440s back to 881,000 BCE. Its relativistic drift was calibrated against DE441 through JPL Horizons. Earth's axis was precessed and checked against Berger & Loutre (1991), and anchor stars were moved with their Hipparcos space motions.
  • Tectonics. India-plate motion from NNR-MORVEL56; true polar wander and axial tilt tested separately.

What it can and cannot say

The method tests consistency. It cannot prove that events happened, and it does not date the poem. The Moon's position, the calendar day and the time of day are beyond computation for the Treta epoch. Both editions list their limits in full.
